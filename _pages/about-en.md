How do scientists in Portuguese-speaking countries understand — and practise —
science communication?

That question organises my work. I am a PhD candidate in Sociology at the
Federal University of Minas Gerais (PPGS/FAFICH, UFMG), supervised by Prof.
Yurij Castelfranchi, and since 2016 executive secretary at the university's
Directorate of Science Communication — which means I study, from the inside,
a practice I help to run.

> **From September 2026 to May 2027 I am a visiting doctoral researcher at the
> Universidad de Oviedo**, Spain, co-supervised by Prof. Carmelo Polino at the
> CTS (Science, Technology and Society) research group, working on the design
> and validation of the comparative survey at the core of my thesis.

## Research

**Scientists and science communication.** What researchers say about public
communication — their dispositions, motivations and the obstacles they report.
My master's thesis addressed the Brazilian case and produced a
[classification of these profiles](/publications/).

**Peripheral science and the geopolitics of knowledge.** How a scientific
system's position in the international division of scientific labour shapes what
its researchers consider worth communicating — and in which language.

**The Lusophone space as a case.** Brazil, Portugal and Portuguese-speaking
African countries share a language and a colonial history, but almost none of
their material conditions for research. This is the scope of my doctoral thesis.

**Methods.** Computational sociology and quantitative analysis for the social
sciences — comparative surveys, latent class models, R and Python.
